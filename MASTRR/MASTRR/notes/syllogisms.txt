A syllogism gives two or more statements about categories, like "All A are B" and "All B are C", and asks what conclusion necessarily follows. Drawing simple circles for each category helps check which conclusions must hold in EVERY possible arrangement, not just a likely one.

A common mistake is assuming a conclusion is true because it "sounds reasonable" -- a valid syllogism conclusion must be guaranteed by the premises in all cases, with no exceptions possible.

When two premises use "some," the safest habit is to assume the conclusion does NOT necessarily follow, unless you can prove it holds in every possible diagram.
